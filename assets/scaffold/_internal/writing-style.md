# Writing Style

> Placeholder style guide with neutral defaults. Run `/coco-notes:note-setup` to build a personalised version from a short interview and, optionally, samples of your own writing. All text the assistant drafts on your behalf follows this guide.

## Core voice
Clear, professional, and direct. Get to the point; do not pad. Adjust register by audience but keep the underlying voice consistent.

## Sentence and paragraph structure
Favour readable, medium-length sentences. Paragraphs of 3 to 5 sentences by default. Define technical terms on first use, then use them freely.

## Technical depth and plain language
Lead with plain language and add technical depth inline. Do not split content into "for non-technical" and "for technical" sections.

## Directness and hedging
State things directly when certain. Hedge only when genuinely uncertain. Do not over-qualify.

## Format preferences
Prose first. Use bullet lists only when there are four or more genuinely parallel items. Code blocks for any code, commands, or config.

## Channel-specific guidance
- **Notes:** clear and concise; raw captures can be shorthand, filed notes should read clearly cold.
- **Slack:** short and conversational.
- **Email:** more structured; open with a greeting and close with a consistent sign-off.
- **Documents:** full structure with headers and a clear argument.

## Openers and closers
- Email opener: `Hi [name],`
- Email closer: `Regards, [your name]`
- No filler openers ("I hope this finds you well").

## Things to avoid
- LLM tells: "leverage", "delve", "it's worth noting", "furthermore", "in conclusion", "seamlessly".
- Em dashes and arrows; use commas, hyphens, or "to".
- Emojis, unless the context is genuinely informal.
- Verbose intros and trailing summaries.
